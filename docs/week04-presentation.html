What Is User-Centered Design?

ユーザー中心設計とは？

User-centered design is a design process that puts real users, their needs, and their context at the center of every decision — and checks those decisions against evidence, not opinion.

ユーザー中心設計とは、実際のユーザーと、そのニーズ、利用状況を、すべての意思決定の中心に置き、その決定を意見ではなく根拠に照らして確認する、デザインプロセスです。

This is not just "think about the user." It's a formal, repeatable process — which is exactly what an international standard exists to define.

ISO 9241-210: The International Standard

ISO 9241-210：国際規格

ISO 9241-210, "Human-centred design for interactive systems," is the international standard that defines human-centered design as a formal process — not just good intentions.

ISO 9241-210「インタラクティブシステムの人間中心設計」は、人間中心設計を、単なる善意ではなく、正式なプロセスとして定義する国際規格です。

It's widely used across the industry — not because it's clever, but because it gives teams a shared, checkable process instead of relying on individual taste.

The Four Activities

4つの活動

1. Understand

Context of use — who, what task, what environment

2. Specify

User requirements — what the design must achieve

3. Produce

Design solutions — sketches, prototypes, flows

4. Evaluate

Against requirements — does it actually work?

↻ The cycle repeats — evaluation feeds back into understanding, until the requirements are actually met.

Why Iterate?

なぜ反復するのか？

A linear process assumes you got the requirements right the first time. You almost never do.

Without iteration

You ship your first guess, and find out it's wrong from angry users — or an agent that confidently does the wrong thing

With iteration

"Done" means evaluation evidence says so — not that you ran out of time to doubt yourself

Context of Use: Who, What, Where

利用状況：誰が、何を、どこで

Activity 1 of ISO 9241-210 asks three questions before any design work starts:

Who

Users, their goals, abilities, and limits — this is where our two Human Factor sessions feed in directly

What

The tasks they're actually trying to accomplish

Where

The environment — noisy, quiet, on the move, hands full

From Context to Personas

利用状況からペルソナへ

"Understand the context of use" is abstract. Teams need something concrete to design against and argue about.

That's what a persona is for.

What Is a Persona?

ペルソナとは？

A persona is a specific, fictional-but-realistic user, built from real research, used to represent a group of actual users with similar goals and behavior.

ペルソナとは、実際の調査に基づいて作られた、具体的で、架空だが現実的なユーザーであり、似た目標と行動を持つ実際のユーザー群を代表するものです。

Popularized in the late 1990s by designer Alan Cooper. The point isn't the fictional name — it's designing for "Hana, who checks the bus app once a minute while late for class" instead of designing for "users" in the abstract.

A Good Persona vs. a Bad Persona

良いペルソナと悪いペルソナ

Bad: a stock photo with a name

  • No research behind it — just assumptions
  • Generic goals ("wants a good experience")
  • Everyone forgets it exists by week two

Good: a research-based tool

  • Built from interviews, observation, or data
  • Specific goals and specific frustrations
  • Used to settle real design arguments: "would Hana understand this?"

Personas for AI and Agent Interfaces

AI・エージェントインターフェースのためのペルソナ

When your interface involves an AI agent, a persona needs one more dimension that a classic persona often skips.

Classic dimensions

Goals, frustrations, context of use, tech comfort level

Add: AI disposition

Do they trust AI too much, too little, or about right? Have they been burned by a bad AI experience before?

People interact with interfaces in three basic ways — direct manipulation, conversation, delegation. Which one does your persona default to, and why?

Today's Activity: Begin Your Persona

今日のアクティビティ：ペルソナを開始する

What You'll Do Today | 今日やること

  1. Pick a real target user group for an interface you care about
  2. Draft a persona: name, one-line summary, 2–3 goals, 2–3 frustrations, context of use, one representative quote
  3. Add the AI disposition dimension from the last slide
  4. Note what it's based on — interviews, observation, or assumptions you still need to check

This is a draft, not a finished persona. You'll complete and validate it next session, alongside an accessibility audit.
