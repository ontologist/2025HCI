What Are Human Factors?

ヒューマンファクターとは？

Human factors (also called ergonomics) is the scientific study of how people's physical and mental characteristics should shape the design of tools, interfaces, and systems.

ヒューマンファクター（人間工学）は、人の身体的・精神的特性が、道具やインターフェース、システムの設計にどう反映されるべきかを研究する学問です。

Three Kinds of Characteristics | 3種類の特性

Body size, reach, strength

Physiological

Vision, hearing, touch, reflexes — today's focus

Cognitive

Memory, attention, mental models — next session

人間の情報処理ループ

2. Process | 処理

3. Act | 行動

Hands, voice, or eyes carry out a response

Today we focus on steps 1 and 3 — the sensing and the acting — because those are governed by physical and physiological limits that every interface has to respect.

How We Sense Interfaces

インターフェースをどう感知するか

👁️ Vision | 視覚

  • ~200° field of view, but sharp detail only in a small central area
  • Peripheral vision is great at noticing motion, poor at reading text
  • Design rule: put critical info centrally, use motion for alerts

👂 Hearing | 聴覚

  • Range: roughly 20 Hz–20 kHz; most sensitive around 2–5 kHz
  • That 2–5 kHz band is also where speech consonants live
  • Design rule: notification tones and voice agents should sit in that range, with captions as a fallback

✋ Touch | 触覚

  • Our fastest-reacting sense (more on the next slide)
  • Haptic buzz confirms an action without needing to look
  • Design rule: use vibration to confirm something happened, especially for agents acting in the background

Reaction Time: How Fast Can You Respond?

反応時間：どれくらい速く反応できるか

Touch

~150 ms

Hearing

~160 ms

Vision

~220 ms

Choices Slow You Down: Hick's Law | ヒックの法則

RT = a + b · log₂(n + 1)

More options (n) means a longer decision time — but only logarithmically, so a well-organized menu of 20 items is much faster than its raw count suggests.

Motor Control and Fitts's Law

運動制御とフィッツの法則

MT = a + b · log₂(2D / W)

Movement time grows with Distance to a target and shrinks with its Width.

移動時間は、ターゲットまでの距離が長くなるほど増え、ターゲットの幅が広くなるほど減ります。

What This Means for Design | デザインへの意味

  • Frequent actions should be big and close
  • Screen edges and corners act like infinitely wide targets — your cursor can't overshoot them
  • Voice sidesteps Fitts's Law entirely — there's nothing to point at, which is one reason voice agents can feel faster for some tasks

System Response Time: Three Limits

システム応答時間：3つの限界

0.1 second — feels instant | 瞬時に感じる

No special feedback needed; the result just appears.

1.0 second — flow stays unbroken | 流れが途切れない

You notice the delay, but your train of thought survives.

10 seconds — the limit of attention | 注意の限界

Beyond this, show a progress indicator or users assume it's frozen.

For AI agents: an agent that "thinks" for 5–10 seconds needs visible progress, or it just looks broken — even though nothing is wrong.

Physical Fatigue and Ergonomics

身体的疲労と人間工学

The Classic Problems | 従来の問題

  • Repeated small movements → repetitive strain
  • Long screen time → eye strain
  • Poor posture → neck and back pain

A New One: Supervising Agents | 新しい問題：エージェントの監督

  • Watching an AI agent work is a different kind of tiring — sustained, low-engagement attention
  • Psychologists call this a vigilance decrement: attention quality drops the longer you passively watch
  • Design response: don't ask people to watch silently — give them natural checkpoints to confirm or redirect

Designing for Physical Diversity

身体的多様性のためのデザイン

Not everyone has the same vision, hearing, or motor ability — and not every situation does either.

Permanent

Low vision, limited mobility, deafness

Situational

One hand full, a loud room, a cracked screen

This is just a preview — we go deep on accessibility and universal design in a future session. Keep it in mind as you do today's audit.

Bringing This to AI and Agent Interfaces

AI・エージェントインターフェースへの応用

Classic interfaces

  • Fitts's Law governs button size and placement
  • Feedback is usually instant (under 0.1s)
  • You watch and act continuously

Agent interfaces

  • Voice sidesteps Fitts's Law; touch targets still matter in agent control panels
  • Delays often exceed 1s — progress feedback is not optional
  • You supervise, which brings vigilance decrement, not muscle fatigue

Same human body, new kind of interface — the same laws still apply, they just show up differently.

Today's Activity: Human Factors Audit

今日のアクティビティ：ヒューマンファクター監査

What You'll Do | やること
