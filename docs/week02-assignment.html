Week 2: Human Factors Audit

Physical and Physiological Characteristics, Applied to a Real Interface

第2週：ヒューマンファクター監査 — 身体的・生理学的特性を実際のインターフェースに適用する

📋 Assignment Overview / 課題概要

In this assignment, you'll audit a real interface — an app, a device, or an AI assistant — against what we covered in Session 2: vision, hearing, and touch; reaction time; Fitts's Law and motor control; and system response time. You'll then propose one concrete redesign based on your findings.

この課題では、実際のインターフェース（アプリ、デバイス、またはAIアシスタント）を、第2回で学んだ視覚・聴覚・触覚、反応時間、フィッツの法則と運動制御、システム応答時間に照らして監査します。その結果に基づいて、具体的な再デザイン案をひとつ提案します。

🎯 Learning Objectives / 学習目標

  • Apply Fitts's Law to estimate the difficulty of a real on-screen or voice interaction
  • Measure or estimate a system's response time against the 0.1s / 1.0s / 10s thresholds
  • Identify which senses (vision, hearing, touch) an interface relies on, and whether it offers a fallback
  • Connect a specific human-factors principle to a concrete redesign recommendation
  • Practice the course's AI transparency habit if you use an AI assistant for any part of this work

📝 Activity Description / アクティビティ説明

Step 1: Choose Your Interface (選択)

  • Pick one app, device, or AI assistant you use regularly
  • At least one of your three good/bad examples from Startup Week is a good candidate — reuse one if it fits
  • If you choose an AI assistant or chatbot, note that explicitly — the questions below apply a little differently to it

Step 2: Time One Key Interaction (計測)

  • Pick the single action you perform most often in this interface (e.g. sending a message, tapping "like," starting a search)
  • Estimate Fitts's Law's two inputs for it: how far you have to reach or move (D), and how big the target is (W). You don't need exact pixel measurements — a reasonable estimate (small / medium / large target, short / long reach) is fine
  • Time how long the interface takes to respond after you act, and compare it against the three limits from class: 0.1s (instant), 1.0s (flow stays unbroken), 10s (limit of attention)
MT = a + b · log₂(2D / W)

Step 3: Map the Senses (感覚のマッピング)

  • List which senses the interface uses to communicate with you: vision, hearing, touch, or some combination
  • For each one, ask: if I couldn't use this sense right now (too loud, too dark, hands full, hard of hearing), would the interface still work?
  • Note any fallback it already has — captions, vibration, high-contrast text — or the lack of one

Step 4: Propose One Redesign (再デザイン)

  • Name one specific principle from class — Fitts's Law, Hick's Law, a response-time limit, or a sensory fallback — that your interface violates or could use better
  • Describe one concrete change that would fix it. "Make it more accessible" is too vague — say what you'd actually change and why, using what you learned today

📦 Deliverables / 提出物

Human Factors Audit Write-Up

Format: PDF document (2–3 pages) or slides

Required Components:
  • Interface chosen: name and one screenshot or photo
  • Fitts's Law estimate: the action you timed, your D and W estimates, and what that implies
  • Response time: your measured or estimated time, and which of the three limits it falls into
  • Sensory map: which senses the interface uses and whether a fallback exists for each
  • Redesign recommendation: the one principle you're applying and the specific change you propose
  • AI transparency log (if used): tool, date, what you asked, what you kept or changed, how you checked it

🤖 AI Use on This Assignment / この課題でのAI使用

Using an AI assistant for this assignment is allowed and expected to be useful — for example, to double-check your Fitts's Law math, to brainstorm redesign ideas, or to tighten your writing.

If you use one, log it: which tool, when, what you asked, what you kept or changed, and how you checked it. This is the same transparency habit from Startup Week, and it applies to every assignment in this course, not just this one.

💡 Tips for Success / 成功のためのヒント

  • Pick something you actually use: it's easier to notice real friction in a familiar interface
  • Estimates are fine: you don't need a lab — "the button looks about this big, and my thumb travels about this far" is a legitimate Fitts's Law estimate
  • Be specific in your redesign: name the principle and the exact change, not a general wish for "better design"
  • If you chose an AI assistant: pay attention to the delay between asking and answering — that's your response-time measurement
